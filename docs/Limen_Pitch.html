The problem

A budget in a prompt is not a budget.

Every agent that can spend money needs a limit. Almost every implementation puts that limit inside the trust boundary of the thing it is meant to constrain.

Where the limit usually lives

A config value, a database column, or a sentence in a system prompt.

All three sit in the same process the model influences.

Why that fails quietly

Nothing rejects the transaction. The money moves and the log looks ordinary.

Nobody finds out until reconciliation, which is days or weeks later.

What is actually needed

A limit the agent can read, obey, and be refused by, but cannot edit.

That means it has to live outside the agent's process entirely.

What Limen is

Four hands on one purchase. Only one of them is the agent.

Enforced, not drawn. Every restricted action is checked server-side against a signed token. Hiding a button is not the same thing as withholding a permission, and the test suite sends each role at every other role's routes to prove it.

Limen · Pitch07
Proof, not a promise · Attack 3

Then we let each person try to do somebody else's job.

Every check runs over HTTP with a real signed token, because testing a guard function proves the function works, not that the route calls it.

Refused by the server
  • Sales tries to release the payment
  • Sales tries to approve its own request
  • The head tries to release the payment
  • Finance tries to pay an unapproved purchase
  • A token edited to claim another role
And the approval does not travel
  • Change the amount after approval, payment refused
  • Change the supplier after approval, payment refused
  • Contract refuses, and the payment provider is called zero times
The last one matters most. An approval is bound to a fingerprint of the supplier, the line, the price and the delivery. Move any of them and the signature stops authorising anything. Tokens are workspace-scoped too.
Limen · Pitch08
Where the AI sits

The model phrases. It never computes, and it never signs.

Stage 1 · Grounded

Every figure is computed deterministically from a frozen projection of the run, and cites its source.

The same request produces the same transcript every time.

Stage 2 · Phrasing only

The model may not introduce a number, name, date or claim. It may not remove a figure that is present.

Validated on shape, length and characters before use.

Capability boundary

The reasoning module has zero imports. No chain, no signer, no network.

Enforced by what the code can reach, not by instructions in a prompt.

The product runs with no model key at all. Without one it falls back to a deterministic responder and behaves identically, because the model was never holding anything up.
Limen · Pitch09
The engine

The cheapest supplier is often the wrong one. The agent knows why.

Structural versus negotiable

Every constraint failure is classified before anything is ranked. Price is negotiable. Schedule sometimes is, and only the supplier knows by how much. Grade, minimum order, capacity and certification never are.

Rejected before negotiation

The cheapest listing in the catalogue never receives an offer: wrong grade, minimum order too large, missing certification.

An agent optimising on price alone buys this one.

Bargaining it cannot cheat at

Counterparties hold a private floor price the agent cannot read. An assertion inside the negotiation loop throws if an offer ever exceeds the authorised ceiling, so a pricing bug becomes a crash rather than a quiet overspend.

Default run, three suppliers

One fails on schedule. One is priced out and the agent walks away. One settles at $1,175, which is $75 off list and $25 under budget.

No deal is a valid outcome, and some runs end in one.

Limen · Pitch10
Payments

The contract is asked before the money rail is touched.

1 · Workflow gate

Right state, right role, and the approval still matching the purchase in front of us.

2 · The contract

Run against the real EVM without sending a transaction. If it reverts, we stop here and the rail is never contacted.

3 · The payout

Created under a key derived from the purchase and its approval, so a timeout cannot pay a supplier twice.

4 · The lifecycle

Processing until a signed webhook says otherwise. Settled, failed or reversed comes from the rail, not from us.

A created payout is an accepted instruction, not money in an account. Webhook events are verified over the raw bytes, deduplicated by event id, and never allowed to move a payment backwards out of a terminal state.
